Divide from Denominations

Denominations can be a tough idea for some as they show Christianity isn’t completely united and that we do sometimes disagree on different topics. There have been questions like ‘Is Mary the mother of God?’ or ‘Does the Church or Bible have more authority?’ Those are two topics that have divided the church in the past. Some people have realised this and because of this have completely left traditional Christianity with the ‘New Church Movement’. I am a Reformed Anglican coming from a theologically right winged, conservative perspective which will impact my ideas and thoughts on all of these denominations. I will only be writing about the divide that has been caused by denominations, rather than picking on particular denominations to keep the least amount of criticism.

Primary, Secondary, Tertiary and Quaternary Issues

We would not have denominations if it were not for the issues that Christians disagree on. These issues can be split up into three main sections, and a fourth which won’t exactly split the church but may split the individual.
There are Primary issues which are things like the Trinity, Nicene creed, Jesus being fully human and fully divine, monotheism and the resurrection. These split Christianity and any other belief. Most people from groups like Jehovah’s Witness, Later Day Saints, Oneness Pentecostals, other Religions, atheism + agnostic, and some Progressive Christians (or at least Progressive Christianity will lead you this way if not already) will disagree with the Primary issues, making them not Christian. If you disagree with one of these, please talk to me! I would love to have a conversation with you!
Now the Secondary issues are stuff like the Sola Scriptura, Infant baptism, the Filioque, the Sacraments, the Pope, Church order, Speaking in Tongues, and One True Church. These will split the denominations, being Protestantism (which has a bunch in and of itself), Catholicism, and Orthodox Christianity, all of these being Christian. Protestants believe in Sola Scriptura (Scripture has the most authority), Catholics and Orthodox split during the Great Schism because of the Filioque (My favourite word), Catholics believe in the Pope and them being one true church, and Orthodox also think they are the one true church. Denominations in Protestantism will split on things like infant baptism, Church Order (basically bishop or elders), speaking in tongues, and the sacraments.
Tertiary issues are issues that split the individual churches like Predestination, Gay Marriage, Abortion, Evolution, Worship style, Slavery and end time theology. This can split Anglicanism for example, into Anglo-Reformed, vs Anglo-Catholic. Whether a church is more progressive will also be included a tertiary issue, but I must mention that in a lot of cases, progressive Christianity has become more of a primary issues with people say, ‘God is a cool idea that lets us do social justice’. In doing that denying God’s existence. Some churches will split over worship style as some might believe Hillsong is good to play in church, or Hymns and Psalms alone. This also includes ‘Should we put our hands in the air?’ or ‘Should there be mosh pits?’ or ‘Should worship be more traditional?’. Predestination can split the individual churches as some may be Arminian and other could be Calvinistic.
Issues that will split the person are Quaternary issues. These are issues like Bible translation, Medical decisions, Movies/Games, Food, Public Schools, Political candidates, Private devotion, and cultural customs. Anti-vaxxers won’t use vaccines, KJV readers would like to read the KJV, and some won’t read any other version. People might not watch some films as it could lead to sin. Parents might not send their kids to public schools. All of these decisions will not effect the church or denomination although it is more personal to the Christian themselves.
