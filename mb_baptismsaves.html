Baptism Saves

People have their rebuttals, but I think it is difficult to say baptism doesn’t save after looking at each of these verses. Both 1 Peter 3:21 and Mark 16:16 state the words baptism and save. I will be looking into what others say on why these verses don’t say baptism saves and will be debunking them. As a Reformed Anglican, I found it hard originally to say baptism saves? How does that work with sola fide? Little did I know, the idea that Baptism saves is a very comforting and assuring idea.

Baptism in the Bible

It is common for people to say that Romans 6:3 is using the Greek ‘βαπτισμός’ (baptismos) to mean it’s literal meaning; immersion. They bring up verse like 1 Corinthians 10:2 ‘They were all baptised into Moses in the cloud and in the sea’. Surely this verse isn’t saying they were sacramentally baptised into Moses? If you take this view, you are missing what Paul is saying. Paul refers to baptism, not as immersion, but as the saving force of the pillar guiding the Israelites, and the splitting of the sea (both are from the Holy Spirit). It would not make sense for Paul to say ‘They were immersed into Moses’, so this verse actually adds to the saving power of Baptism. Another thing which is strange of Romans 6:3, is that in the context of ‘Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase?’ Paul goes straight away to baptism. How easy and yet still correct for him to talk about faint, he instead says that through baptism, we are dead to sin and resurrected with Christ!

Baptismal Regeneration in the Anglican Tradtion

In Thomas Cranmer’s Catechism in the 1662 Book of Common Prayer, when it asks how many sacraments are there, it states ‘Two only, as generally necessary for salvation, that is to say, Baptism and the supper of the Lord’. Later, asking for the inward and spiritual grace of Baptism, it responds ‘A death unto sin and a new birth unto righteousness for being by nature, born unto sin, and the children of wrath, we are hereby made children of Grace.’ These show baptism is generally (more on that later) necessary for salvation but also shows exactly what happens when someone is Baptised. We clearly are unable to say that Baptism is just a symbol if you are apart of the Anglican tradition, even though this is what a lot of Sydney Anglicans hold to.
